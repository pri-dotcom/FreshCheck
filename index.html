    Produce Encyclopedia

    Know Your Fruits & Vegetables

    Learn the natural, healthy appearance of common produce, how to recognize early decay, what to look for at the market, and how to store them safely.

    Fruit

    Apple

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Tight, smooth, taut skin with uniform color; heavy for size and firm to gentle thumb pressure.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Soft brown sunken bruises, mealy or mushy flesh, cidery or fermented smell, white core mold.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose unblemished apples with intact stems. Avoid wrinkled skins or soft spots near the base.

    Storage Tip: High ethylene producer. Store chilled in crisper drawer away from greens.
    Fruit

    Banana

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Even bright yellow peel. Tiny brown speckles ("sugar spots") indicate peak natural sweetness.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Entirely black skin weeping moisture, white fungal mold at stem crown, foul sour fermentation smell.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Select greenish-yellow clusters with intact stem crowns and no deep black split gashes.

    Storage Tip: Keep at room temperature. Wrap stems with foil or plastic to slow ethylene release.
    Fruit

    Orange

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Firm, vibrant orange rind with fine textured pores; feels heavy and juicy in hand.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Greenish-blue or white powdery mold (Penicillium), soft spongy waterlogged depressions.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Look for bright smooth skin free of dull patches or soft discolored spots around the stem navel.

    Storage Tip: Countertop for 1 week; refrigerate in a mesh bag for up to 3-4 weeks.
    Fruit

    Mango

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Plump, taut skin with sweet fragrant aroma near the stem; gives slightly to gentle pressure.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Large black sunken anthracnose rot patches, alcoholic smell, leaking fluid, excessively mushy skin.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Focus on sweet fragrance and plumpness rather than color alone; avoid bruised or shriveled skin.

    Storage Tip: Ripen at room temperature; once soft and ripe, refrigerate for up to 5 days.
    Fruit

    Grapes

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Plump, firm berries firmly attached to pliable green stems; natural silvery powdery bloom.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Grey fuzzy mold (Botrytis), sticky shriveled brown berries, loose berries falling off dry brittle stems.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Check the bottom of the bag for moisture or loose berries. Ensure stems are green, not brown.

    Storage Tip: Do not wash before storing. Keep in original ventilated container in the fridge.
    Fruit

    Papaya

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Golden-yellow skin with slight green blush; firm yet yields gently under palm pressure.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Sunken dark brown circular lesions, white fuzzy mold spots, fermented vinegar odor, collapsing skin.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Avoid papayas with bruised patches or dark moisture spots. Choose evenly colored yellow-orange fruit.

    Storage Tip: Store unripe papaya at room temperature. Once ripe, chill in fridge for 3-4 days.
    Fruit

    Guava

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Light green to yellow skin, firm flesh, pleasant floral aroma, free of deep blemishes.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Soft brown waterlogged depressions, pungent over-fermented odor, fruit fly holes, skin collapse.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose unblemished guavas with a strong fresh tropical scent. Avoid overly soft or dented fruits.

    Storage Tip: Store at room temperature until ripe, then refrigerate in a paper bag for 2-3 days.
    Fruit

    Pomegranate

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Firm, heavy, smooth-skinned rind ranging from deep red to pink or yellowish. Crown end intact and dry.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Soft spongy spots, dark sunken patches, moldy crown, shriveled skin, dried-out lightweight feel, fermented off-odor.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose the heaviest pomegranate for its size with a taut, unblemished rind. Avoid fruits with cracks or sunken areas.

    Storage Tip: Store whole at room temperature for up to 2 weeks. Refrigerate for up to 2 months in an airtight bag.
    Fruit

    Watermelon

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Dark green striped or solid skin, creamy yellow field spot on underside, symmetrical shape, dull matte surface.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Soft sunken patches, foul sour odor, slimy skin surface, cracks with leaking fluid, white or black mold spots.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Knock on the rind — a hollow thud indicates ripeness. The yellow ground spot should be creamy, not white or green.

    Storage Tip: Keep whole at room temperature away from sunlight. Once cut, cover tightly and refrigerate.
    Fruit

    Pineapple

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Golden-yellow base with slightly green crown leaves. Sweet tropical fragrance at the base. Firm but slightly yielding eyes.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Dark water-soaked patches at base, mushy soft eyes, fermented alcoholic odor, brown slimy crown leaves.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Smell the base for sweetness. Tug a center leaf — if it comes out easily, the pineapple is ripe. Avoid dark base stains.

    Storage Tip: Store uncut at room temperature for 1-2 days, then refrigerate. Cut pineapple keeps for up to 5 days.
    Fruit

    Sapota (Chikoo)

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Brown, rough, potato-like skin that yields gently to pressure when ripe. Smooth and even surface free of cracks.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Fermented alcoholic odor, overly soft mushy flesh, skin cracks with leaking sticky fluid, white mold patches.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Squeeze gently — ripe sapota yields uniformly. Avoid fruits with hard green patches or sunken soft spots.

    Storage Tip: Ripen at room temperature. Once ripe, refrigerate for a few days. Keep away from strong-smelling foods.
    Fruit

    Sweet Lime (Mosambi)

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Smooth, pale greenish-yellow to light yellow rind. Heavy for size with high juice content. Firm, unblemished skin.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Blue-green Penicillium mold patches, soft spongy spots, dried shrunken rind, bitter fermented odor.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose heavy, blemish-free fruits with tight smooth skin. Avoid fruits with white powder residue or sunken pores.

    Storage Tip: Store at room temperature for 3-4 days or refrigerate in a mesh bag for up to 2 weeks.
    Fruit

    Lemon

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Bright, vibrant yellow rind (or slightly green for firm lemons). Smooth, firm, heavy for size indicating good juice content.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    White or blue-green fuzzy mold (Penicillium), soft spongy patches, shriveled wrinkled rind, foul sour-mold odor.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose firm lemons heavy for their size with glossy, smooth skin. Avoid lemons with thick, puffy, soft rinds.

    Storage Tip: Store loose in the refrigerator in a mesh bag. Keeps for 3-4 weeks when refrigerated properly.
    Fruit

    Muskmelon (Kharbuja)

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Creamy beige to pale orange netted skin. Sweet musky fragrance at the blossom end. Slight softening at blossom end when ripe.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Dark sunken soft spots, slimy netted skin surface, strong fermented alcoholic odor, cracks with leaking fluid.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Smell the blossom end for a sweet musky aroma. The rind should feel slightly rough and netted without cracks.

    Storage Tip: Ripen at room temperature. Once soft and fragrant, refrigerate and consume within 3-5 days.
    Vegetable

    Tomato

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Glossy, taut skin, bright uniform red color, firm yet yields slightly; fresh earthy vine aroma.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Black blossom-end rot on bottom, weeping soft wrinkles, white or green fuzzy mold spots, sour odor.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Select heavy, plump tomatoes with fresh green calyx stems. Avoid bruised or split skins.

    Storage Tip: Store stem-down at room temperature. Avoid refrigeration to preserve flavor.
    Vegetable

    Potato

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Rock-hard, dry skin, earthy neutral smell, free of green patches and vigorous sprouting eyes.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Green skin (toxic solanine), extensive sprouts, soft spongy zones, foul or musty bacterial odor.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Ensure tubers are solid and smooth without cuts, dark soil caked over bruises, or greenish tint.

    Storage Tip: Store in a cool, dark, well-ventilated basket. Keep far away from onions.
    Vegetable

    Onion

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Dry, papery outer skin that crackles; firm, heavy bulb with tight dry neck and no sprouting shoots.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Black powdery mold (Aspergillus niger) between layers, soft mushy neck, weeping liquid, foul odor.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Gently squeeze the stem neck to verify firmness. Reject onions with soft spots or wet layers.

    Storage Tip: Store in dry open mesh bags in a cool pantry. Never store inside plastic bags.
    Vegetable

    Brinjal (Eggplant)

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Smooth, glossy, deep purple skin; firm to the touch with a fresh green stem cap.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Dull wrinkled skin, soft brown mushy spots, waterlogged sunken areas, brown seeds inside.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Look for bright shiny skin without scars. Stem should be green and free of white mold fuzz.

    Storage Tip: Best stored at 10-12°C. Keep in refrigerator crisper for up to 4-5 days.
    Vegetable

    Carrot

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Rigid, bright orange root that snaps cleanly; smooth surface without rubbery bend.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Slimy surface coating (bacterial biofilm), black rot on crown, rubbery flexible texture, mold.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose firm, brightly colored carrots. Avoid carrots with green sunburnt tops or cracks.

    Storage Tip: Remove leafy green tops immediately. Keep in a sealed container in the fridge.
    Vegetable

    Cabbage

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Compact, heavy, dense head with crisp, tightly packed outer leaves and clean stem base.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Black or brown slimy outer leaves, grey mold, foul sulfurous odor, spongy lightweight center.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose heads that feel heavy for their size with crisp, non-wilted outer wrapping leaves.

    Storage Tip: Do not wash before storing. Keep whole in refrigerator crisper for up to 3-4 weeks.
    Vegetable

    Cauliflower

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Clean, creamy-white, tightly packed florets surrounded by crisp, green protective leaves.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Extensive dark brown or black speckles on curd, loose separating florets, slimy texture, sour smell.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Look for solid, heavy white curds. Avoid heads with brown discoloration or yellowish wilting leaves.

    Storage Tip: Store curd-side up in a perforated plastic bag in the refrigerator for 5-7 days.
    Vegetable

    Cucumber

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Firm, dark green, cylindrical body with smooth or slightly bumpy skin. Cool to the touch, heavy with water content.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Yellow or pale skin discoloration, soft spongy spots, slimy skin surface, shriveled wrinkled ends, bitter taste.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose cucumbers that are dark green, firm end-to-end. Avoid yellowish cucumbers or those with soft spots.

    Storage Tip: Wrap in paper towel and store in crisper drawer. Use within 5-7 days. Do not freeze.
    Vegetable

    Capsicum (Bell Pepper)

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Bright, shiny, firm skin in red, green, or yellow. Thick walls with crisp snap. Fresh green stem with no browning.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Soft, wrinkled skin, dark sunken spots, white mold near stem, brown slimy interior, shriveled walls.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose peppers with thick, rigid walls and shiny, unblemished skin. Fresh green stems indicate recent harvest.

    Storage Tip: Refrigerate unwashed in crisper drawer. Red and yellow peppers ripen faster than green; use them first.
    Vegetable

    Okra (Bhindi)

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Bright, vibrant green pods, 5-8 cm long. Tender enough to snap cleanly. Fresh smooth surface without brown patches.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Browning or black spots, slimy mucilaginous surface, soft soggy pods, shriveled dull skin, mold near cut ends.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    The best okra pods snap cleanly without bending. Avoid oversized or yellow pods which are tough and fibrous.

    Storage Tip: Store dry in paper bag in refrigerator. Moisture causes rapid sliming. Use within 3-4 days.
    Vegetable

    Spinach (Palak)

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Vibrant deep green leaves, crisp and firm texture, no visible wilting or yellowing, clean fresh green aroma.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Yellow or brown wilted leaves, slimy wet texture, foul ammonia-like odor, dark mushy patches, visible mold.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Select bunches with deep green, crisp, upright leaves. Avoid pre-bagged spinach with any yellow or wet spots.

    Storage Tip: Store unwashed in an airtight container with a paper towel to absorb moisture. Rinse only before use.
    Vegetable

    Bitter Gourd (Karela)

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Firm, dark or light green warty ridged skin. Bright, fresh color. No yellowing. Hard flesh that does not give to pressure.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Yellow or orange skin (overripe), soft spongy spots, brown mushy patches, shriveled skin, exposed red seeds inside.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Choose medium-sized, firm, deeply ridged bitter gourds without any yellow tinge. The darker green, the better.

    Storage Tip: Refrigerate unwashed in a loosely sealed bag. Use within 3-5 days before it yellows and becomes overripe.
    Vegetable

    Bottle Gourd (Lauki)

    ✅ Healthy Appearance:

    Pale to medium green smooth skin, firm flesh, symmetrical shape, no soft spots. Light fuzz on skin is normal when young.

    ⚠️ Common Spoilage Signs:

    Yellow or brown skin patches, soft spongy spots, foul bitter odor, shriveled ends, sticky sap on cut surfaces.

    🛒 What to Observe While Purchasing:

    Scratch the skin with a fingernail — fresh bottle gourd has tender, moisture-rich skin. Avoid yellowed or hard, dry specimens.

    Storage Tip: Store uncut in a cool dry place for up to a week. Once cut, wrap tightly and refrigerate for 3-4 days.

    Visual Pathology Guide

    How to Identify Spoiled Fruits and Vegetables

    Microorganisms like fungi (molds and yeasts) and bacteria break down cellular plant walls. Here are the 6 visible warning signs to check before preparing or eating produce.

    1. Dark Spots

    Unusual dark or black patches may indicate bruising, physical injury, or deep fungal spoilage. While superficial brown speckles on bananas indicate natural sugar ripeness, sunken dark lesions on apples, tomatoes, or citrus often signal tissue breakdown that invites microbial rot.

    Inspect Depth • Trim Deeply if Surface Only

    2. Visible Mold

    Fuzzy, velvety, or powdery growth in shades of white, blue, green, or grey is a direct indicator of active fungal colonies. Mold spores penetrate soft fruits and produce dangerous mycotoxins. Never taste visibly moldy produce, as inhaling or ingesting mold spores poses respiratory and digestive risks.

    Do Not Taste • Discard Soft Produce

    3. Discoloration

    Unusual color shifts, such as green patches on potatoes (solanine accumulation), yellowing on leafy greens, or dull brown water-soaked blotches on peppers. Discoloration indicates enzymatic oxidation, chlorophyll breakdown, or toxic alkaloid production.

    Discard Green Potatoes • Trim Browning

    4. Soft or Damaged Areas

    Leaking, mushy, collapsed, or severely indented areas are clear signs that bacterial soft rot has dissolved the plant's internal cellular pectin. Leaking fluid indicates that microbial contamination has spread throughout the flesh.

    Immediate Discard • High Bacterial Risk

    5. Wrinkled and Shriveled Skin

    Excessive wrinkling indicates severe dehydration, cell collapse, and extended aging. While slightly wrinkled peppers or apples may remain safe to cook in stews or purees if free from mold, rubbery limpness marks the end of acceptable shelf life.

    Cook Promptly • Discard if Mushy

    6. Unpleasant Smell

    A foul, sour, fermented, or alcoholic smell is an unmistakable warning sign of anaerobic bacterial decomposition or wild yeast fermentation. Important: An uploaded image cannot detect odor, so you must always use your own sense of smell before eating!

    Trust Your Senses • Discard Immediately
    Public Health Practice

    Simple Habits for Safer Food

    Adopt practical food hygiene habits recommended by public health guidelines to protect your family and extend the life of your fresh produce.

    01

    Choose Fresh-Looking Produce

    Select fruits and vegetables that are vibrant, firm, and free of deep bruises, mold, or skin punctures.

    02

    Avoid Visible Mold or Severe Damage

    Never buy or consume soft produce with visible mold fuzz. Spores spread invisibly deep into porous plant tissues.

    03

    Wash Under Clean Running Water

    Always wash produce under running tap water right before preparation. Never wash produce before refrigerator storage.

    04

    Keep Raw and Ready-to-Eat Foods Separate

    Use separate cutting boards and knives for fresh produce and raw meats to prevent dangerous cross-contamination.

    05

    Store Produce According to Requirements

    Keep ethylene-releasing produce (apples, bananas) away from ethylene-sensitive items (greens, potatoes, carrots).

    06

    Refrigerate Perishables Promptly

    Refrigerate cut, sliced, or peeled produce at or below 4°C (40°F) within two hours of preparation.

    07

    Remove Damaged Produce Promptly

    Immediately discard any decaying fruit from your fruit bowl. One rotting item accelerates spoilage for all nearby produce.

    08

    Keep Storage Areas & Utensils Clean

    Sanitize crisper drawers, refrigerator shelves, cutting boards, and knives regularly to stop mold spore transfer.

    🛒

    Before You Buy: Smart Market Shopping Tips

    Simple inspection habits to help you choose high-quality produce at the market or grocery store:

    👀 1. Inspect Stems & Crowns

    Check the stem end of melons, squash, and oranges. A brown, shriveled, or weeping stem indicates old stock.

    ⚖️ 2. Feel the Weight

    Fresh citrus, apples, and tomatoes should feel heavy for their size, indicating high juice content and crisp cellular turgor.

    👃 3. Smell the Blossom End

    Fresh mangoes, melons, and tomatoes have an earthy, sweet aroma. Avoid items with a sour, fermented, or musty smell.

    📦 4. Check the Container Bottom

    When buying pre-packaged berries or grapes, always inspect the bottom of the carton for squashed fruits or damp fungal juice.

    Community Service Project

    About FreshCheck

    PROJECT TITLE:

    AI-Based Detection of Unsafe Fruits and Vegetables

    Project Type: Community Service Project (CSP)

    🎯 Project Objectives

    1. Identify visible signs of fruit and vegetable spoilage through client-side visual inspection.
    2. Improve food-safety awareness among the general public, students, and community members.
    3. Provide easy image-based visual screening accessible on any smartphone or browser.
    4. Promote safer food-handling practices to prevent avoidable household foodborne illnesses.
    5. Educate people about identifying damaged, spoiled, and unwholesome produce.

    🔬 Project Approach & Methodology

    The website uses basic image-processing techniques to examine the external visual characteristics of fruits and vegetables. By converting uploaded pixels into RGB luminance and HSV/HSL color distributions, it deterministically detects necrotic lesions, browning oxidation, and fungal spore patches.

    It provides an initial visual screening result and educational guidance. The system is designed strictly as a public awareness and educational tool, running 100% on the client device without sending images to external cloud servers, and does not guarantee food safety.
